Investment memo and MIS analysis for a venture fund

Two tasks: write an investment memo on an early-stage wealth-tech startup, and analyse a portfolio company's MIS and balance sheet ahead of a board meeting.

FirmKalaari Capital
SectorWealth-tech, SaaS
WhenNovember 2026
Format23-page memo & analysis

Part 1: Investment memo

The company manages global portfolios for Indian investors, funded through the RBI's Liberalised Remittance Scheme. My thesis: Indian households now save through markets, offshore is the next allocation, and as access becomes a commodity, value goes to whoever owns the advice relationship and the foreign-asset tax workflow.

  • Top-down and bottom-up market sizing, and the assets the company must reach for a 3× return.
  • Competitor benchmarks on growth, margins and asset yield.
  • Per-client unit economics at three ticket sizes, showing where the model makes and loses money.
  • Regulatory licences needed, a ranked risk register, and recommended deal terms with a price ceiling.
  • Seven exit scenarios with ownership, dilution, MOIC and IRR.

Part 2: MIS and balance-sheet analysis

For a SaaS company, I rebuilt the story from the numbers: contracted ARR recovered, but net retention of about 65% caps ARR well below break-even, and runway is about 15 months. I also found formula errors and manual adjustments in the MIS, and wrote the questions and data requests for the board meeting.

What it shows: I can read a business through its numbers — unit economics, retention and cash — and turn findings into a clear recommendation and the questions that test it.
