Ola Seat: a guaranteed electric seat for India's daily commute

The brief: a product that gets 100 million Indians a better daily commute by 2035. My answer: sell a seat on a schedule, not a ride on demand.

CompanyOla Electric
Role appliedAssociate PM
WhenOctober 2026
Format5-page product strategy

The problem

For a 6–20 km commute every option fails on something. Two-wheelers are cheap but account for 45% of road deaths. Buses and shared autos are slow and unreliable, metros stop short of most homes, and cabs cost 3–4× a bike. India defaults to the motorbike.

The product

Ola Seat is a monthly-pass, reserved-seat micro-transit network running on a purpose-built 7-seat electric "Ola Pod". Riders commit to a home-to-work window; neighbours on the same corridor are clustered; routes are planned weekly from pass data. At about ₹1,730 a month for a 12 km commute, it costs less than owning a bike and about a fifth of a cab, with vetted drivers, CCTV, SOS and women-first seating.

Key insight: commitment is what makes shared transport cheap. The pass is the demand signal, dense demand fills seats, and full seats make the pass cheaper than a bike.

What the assignment covers

  • Alternatives rejected: personal micro-EVs, robotaxis, pooled cabs, buses and e-3-wheelers, scored on reach, cost, external dependency and fit with Ola.
  • Unit economics: ₹10.5 cost per vehicle-km against ₹12 revenue at 4 riders; break-even at 3.5 of 7 seats, with sensitivity to price, pod cost and autonomy.
  • Path to 100M: ~2.5M pods, staged Prove → Build → Blanket phases with gates on retention, load and cost.
  • Riskiest assumptions first: a 90-day plan to pre-sell passes through employers before buying a single vehicle.
  • Metrics: North Star of Daily Active Commuters, with habit, reliability, efficiency, trust and guardrail metrics.
