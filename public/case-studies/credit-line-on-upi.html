Launching Credit Line on UPI and credit cards with banks

At Vegapay, a credit infrastructure company, I led end-to-end integration and rollout of Credit Line on UPI and credit card programs for banks and fintech partners.

RoleFounder's Office
CompanyVegapay
WhenFeb – Oct 2026
Scale15+ go-lives, 6+ banks & partners

Context

Credit Line on UPI lets a customer pay with a pre-approved bank credit line through any UPI app. For a bank, launching it means connecting the lending stack (loan origination, business rules, loan management, repayments) to UPI networks and partner apps — each with its own specs, certification and timelines. Card programs carry the same kind of multi-party integration.

The problem

Every launch involved the bank, Vegapay's product and engineering teams, network and ecosystem partners. Requirements arrived in each partner's own format, UAT dragged, and issues in production pulled in many teams at once. Launches took too long, and each one felt like the first.

What I did

  • Turned business asks into API-led solutions. I translated each partner's requirements into scalable, API-based solution designs the product and engineering teams could reuse across banks.
  • Owned launches end to end. For 15+ production implementations across 6+ banks and partners I owned requirements, solution design, API integrations, UAT, go-live and post-production stabilisation.
  • Built release readiness and governance. I set up implementation governance and release-readiness checks driven by operational metrics, so launch risks surfaced before go-live instead of after.
  • Ran incident response. When card programs hit production issues, I coordinated the cross-functional teams and business stakeholders to restore service and keep the business running.
Key idea: a launch is a product too. Treating the implementation process as something to design, measure and improve is what made launches faster, not working harder on each one.

Results

  • 30% shorter implementation turnaround for Credit Line on UPI and credit card programs
  • 30% lower MTTR on production incidents in card programs
  • 25% fewer production incidents after release-readiness governance
  • 15+ go-lives across 6+ banks and partners

What I learned

In B2B2C fintech, the slowest party sets the pace. The biggest gains came from making requirements and readiness explicit early, so the bank, partners and our own engineers were aligned on what "ready" meant before UAT started.
